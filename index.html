How it works

The data

600 houses are generated in your browser with a fixed random seed, so the page gives the same results every visit. Price depends on area, bedrooms, location and age, plus random noise. Downtown is the baseline location, so Rural, Suburb and Waterfront appear as separate yes/no features. A fifth of the houses are held back for testing.

The three models

Linear regression finds the coefficients that minimize squared error. Ridge adds a penalty on the sum of squared coefficients. Lasso adds a penalty on the sum of absolute coefficients. A larger alpha means a stronger penalty. Numeric features are standardized first so the penalty treats them fairly.

What to try

Set training houses to 30 and switch on the irrelevant features. Linear regression now fits noise and loses accuracy on unseen houses. Tuned Lasso beats it by dropping the useless features, while Ridge, which only shrinks them, helps less. With all 480 training houses and no extra features, the three models are nearly identical, which is the honest result for a small linear problem.

The data is synthetic, so prices are in plain units and not tied to a real city.
